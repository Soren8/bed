Build a king bed like furniture: firm core, soft top, simple base.

A firm foam core holds you up. A soft topper — thick 4″ for deep sink or thin 1–2″ for a lighter touch — makes it comfortable. A plain cover holds it together. A floor-supported 2×4 base holds it off the floor. Every part is replaceable — that is the whole trick, and why my ~$200 stack (unwanted firm mattress, 2×4 retrofit to the existing frame, cheap 4″ Amazon topper) is still my best-bed reference.

Why modular wins: the $200 lesson

My reference stack cost about $200: an unwanted firm mattress I already had, a 2×4 retrofit to strengthen the existing frame, and a cheap 4″ Amazon topper on top. It is my best-bed anecdote because the jobs were separated — the firm layer did the supporting, the soft layer did the comfort.

A brand-new king costs more today, but the logic holds. Below we price new king stacks the same way: firm core + soft top + cover (unless built in) + base. Prices and stock are the Sept 14, 2026 snapshot — check the linked listing before you commit.

The comfort layer

Soft toppers: thick 4″ plush or a thinner 1–2″ touch

A “true 4″” topper means roughly four inches of soft foam — not two inches of foam plus two inches of loft. That distinction matters below. But 4″ was only ever a suggestion: a thinner 1–2″ soft layer keeps more of the firm core’s feel and is often — but not always — cheaper (compare: Linenspa 2″ $79.99 costs more than Sweetcrispy 4″ $74.93). Material and firmness matter more than thickness alone: memory foam (EGO, Linenspa) sinks and hugs, while Super Soft conventional polyurethane (12 ILD) is a springier plush layer. If you like a gentle cushion rather than a deep sink, start thin — you can always go thicker later.

The base

A dead-simple floor-supported 2×4 king base

Nineteen 2×4×8s, screws, and patience. Rails and the center beam sit on the floor — no legs to wobble, no engineering promises beyond “measure twice, screw it square.” Buy 96″ boards at the store, not pre-cut 92⅝″ studs.

Cut list — 19 boards, all 2×4×8

QtyPartCutNotes
2Side rails80″On edge, floor-supported
2End rails73″Fit between sides → 76″ outside
1Center beam77″Fits between ends, on edge, floor-supported
14Slats76″Laid flat across rails + center

Math check: slats at 3½″ × 14 = 49″; 31″ left over 13 gaps ≈ 2.39″ each. Each slat bears on two sides + center, so spans are ≈ 35.75″. Screw the frame square, add blocking from offcuts, smooth all wood. This is a shop project, not structural-engineering guidance and carries no load promises.

How to read this page honestly

Methodology

  • Research synthesis, not hands-on testing of every combination. We compared listings, specs, seller-hosted reviews, and return/shipping terms.
  • Outbound links are ordinary links (rel="noopener").
  • Totals are arithmetic on snapshot prices. Your total moves with lumber, tax, shipping, and stock.

Fine print that actually matters

  • Foam Factory: restrictive returns, no generous sleep trial; free contiguous-US shipping on qualifying $100+ orders.
  • Bare foam + fabric cover is not verified here as a fire-tested finished mattress.
  • Density (lb/ft³) and firmness (ILD) are different numbers — don’t conflate them.
  • Wood project: smooth all surfaces, screw square, support rails and center on the floor. Not engineered load guidance.
