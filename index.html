Longevity logic: the core must outlive the comfort

Core = support

Support. Must outlive everything in the stack.

Topper = comfort

Comfort. Disposable by design; replace it.

Cover = holds together

Holds everything together. No support job.

Base = wood

Wood slats on the floor. Lasts forever.

How long materials last

  • Natural latex 12–20 yr — longest.
  • Pocket-coil support, durable — hybrid tracks its comfort layer (~7–12 yr).
  • High-density foam ~6–10 yr — durable for foam.
  • Budget 1.5–1.8 PCF polyfoam ~3–6 yr — shortest.

Ranges summarized from Sleep Foundation, Tom’s Guide, and the Avocado help center. Ranges, not guarantees; comfort layers die first in every construction.

Why foam-on-foam was backwards: a 1.8 PCF core ages at the same rate as the topper, so the whole stack sags together. When foam still makes sense: as the cheapest experiment — a 2.8 PCF HQ core with a thin replaceable topper.

DIY, method & watchlist

2×4 base plan — 19 boards, $72–$120

Cut list

  • 2 side rails — 80″ (on edge)
  • 2 end rails — 73″ (between sides)
  • 1 center beam — 77″ (on edge)
  • 14 slats — 76″ (flat, gaps ≈2.39″)

Buy 96″ boards, not 92⅝″ studs. Slat spans ≈35.75″. Shop project, not engineering guidance.

Options

  • Reuse base — $0 if flat & supportive
  • Retrofit slats — $60–$85 est.
  • Raised base — $100–$150 conceptual, needs legs + bracing

Method & fine print
  • Research synthesis, not hands-on testing of every combo. Firmness feel varies.
  • Outbound links are ordinary links (rel="noopener"). Totals are arithmetic on snapshot prices.
  • Foam Factory: restrictive returns, no generous sleep trial; free contiguous-US shipping on qualifying $100+ orders.
  • Bare foam + fabric cover is not verified here as a fire-tested finished mattress.
  • Density (lb/ft³) and firmness (ILD) are different numbers.
  • Longevity ranges (latex 12–20 yr, hybrid ~7–12 yr, high-density foam ~6–10 yr, budget polyfoam ~3–6 yr) are summarized from Sleep Foundation, Tom’s Guide, and the Avocado help center — ranges, not guarantees.
